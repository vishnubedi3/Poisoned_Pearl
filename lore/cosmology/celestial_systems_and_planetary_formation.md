# Celestial Systems and Planetary Formation

## Source Material

### Patch 1: General Formation
Around stars, remaining gas, dust, and heavier elements formed rotating protoplanetary environments. Within these environments, material gradually accumulated into larger celestial bodies.

In the development of celestial systems, gaseous bodies formed first. These bodies were composed primarily of the gases present in their surrounding environments.

Terrestrial bodies formed later from material remaining within the developing systems, including debris and material produced through collisions and gravitational interactions among existing bodies. In this cosmology, terrestrial bodies therefore came after the gaseous bodies.

A celestial system consists of a principal star and the celestial bodies that orbit it.

### Patch 2: Structure of Celestial Systems and Energy Gradient
Within a celestial system, terrestrial bodies generally occupy the inner planetary regions, while gaseous bodies generally occupy regions farther from the principal star. Beyond these regions lies an increasingly cold outer region, where the energy received from the principal star becomes progressively weaker.

The celestial system therefore contains an energy gradient. At one extreme, excessive stellar energy can create hot wastelands. At the other, insufficient stellar energy can create cold wastelands. Between them lies the range in which stellar energy can support the development and persistence of life.

The principal star remains the sole natural external energy source of the celestial system. Life depends upon its energy, but the amount of energy received must remain within suitable limits. Too little energy prevents life from being sustained. Too much energy can destroy the conditions required for life.

Thus, the star is both the source of life-sustaining energy and, when its energy is received in excessive amounts, a potential source of planetary destruction.

## Organizational Metadata & Relationships

- **Entity Type:** Cosmological Structure & Process
- **Definition:** A celestial system is defined as a principal star and the celestial bodies orbiting it.
- **Formation Chronology:**
  1. **Rotating Protoplanetary Environments:** Formed around stars from remaining gas, dust, and heavier elements.
  2. **Gaseous Bodies:** The first bodies to form, composed primarily of environmental gases. They generally occupy regions farther from the principal star.
  3. **Terrestrial Bodies:** Formed later from remaining material, debris, collisions, and gravitational interactions. They generally occupy the inner planetary regions.
- **Structural Gradient:** 
  - Celestial systems contain an *energy gradient* radiating outward from the principal star.
  - Close proximity = excessive energy (Hot Wasteland / Planetary Destruction).
  - Middle distance = suitable limits (supports development and persistence of life).
  - Far regions = increasingly cold / insufficient energy (Cold Wasteland).
- **Lore Evolution:** Patch 2 introduces the specific spatial arrangement of the system (terrestrial inner, gaseous farther out) and introduces the concept of the energy gradient, linking celestial structure to planetary habitability and the potential for life.
